Maps and local searchGoogle Business Profile checklist for Maui businesses

By Zachary Tye Wennstedt. Published October 9, 2026. About 6 minutes to read.

The setup and monthly upkeep that decide whether you show up when someone on the island searches for what you do.

The short answer: claim and verify your free Google Business Profile, choose the one primary category that matches what you are, use the exact same business name, phone and address everywhere, add real photos, keep hours current (including holidays), and ask every customer for a review. Then check it every month. That upkeep is what keeps a Maui business visible in the map results.

Why the profile matters more on an island

When someone in Kahului searches "plumber near me" or a visitor in Kāʻanapali searches "snorkel rental," Google usually shows a map with a short list of businesses before any regular website results. Those listings come from Google Business Profiles. For many Maui businesses, the profile gets more views than the website does.

Maui also has a few quirks that trip up profiles: Hawaiian place names with the ʻokina and kahakō typed several different ways, addresses that confuse map pins Upcountry and in Hāna, and seasonal hours that change with visitor traffic. Each of those is fixable with a little care.

Setup checklist

  1. Claim or create the profile at Google Business Profile using a Google account you control. Don't let a designer or employee own the only login; add them as managers instead.
  2. Verify it. Google may ask for a video, a phone or email code, or a postcard. Until it's verified, edits may not show publicly.
  3. Use your real business name, exactly as it appears on your signage. Adding keywords or town names that aren't part of your legal or branded name breaks Google's guidelines and can get a profile suspended.
  4. Pick the right primary category. It's the single most important field. Add secondary categories only for services you actually provide.
  5. Address or service area. If customers visit you, show the address and check the pin. If you travel to customers, set up as a service-area business, hide the street address and list the towns you cover, such as Kīhei, Wailea, Wailuku and Makawao.
  6. Phone and website. Use a local or toll-free number you answer, and link to the page that best matches the profile, not always the homepage.
  7. Write the description in plain language: what you do, who you serve and where. No links, no all caps, no promotions.

Name, address and phone: keep them identical

Google cross-checks your details against your website, Yelp, Facebook, TripAdvisor and local directories. Small differences add doubt. Pick one version and use it everywhere, down to "Suite" versus "#" and whether you write Kīhei with the kahakō. If you've moved, which many West Maui businesses have, update every listing, not just Google.

Photos and details that earn clicks

  • Real photos of your storefront, team, work and products. A visitor trying to find you on Front Street or in a Pāʻia shopping center needs to recognize the door.
  • Services or products with short, plain descriptions.
  • Attributes that apply to you, such as wheelchair accessibility, outdoor seating or online appointments.
  • Booking or menu links if you take reservations or serve food.

Reviews: ask everyone, answer everyone

Reviews influence both where you appear and whether people call. Google's rules are simple: don't offer discounts or gifts for reviews, don't write reviews for yourself, and don't ask only your happiest customers. Within those rules, the best habit is to ask every customer the same way, right after the job or visit, with a direct link from your profile's "Ask for reviews" option.

Reply to every review, good or bad, briefly and by name. A calm, specific reply to a complaint often does more for the next reader than five more stars.

Monthly upkeep checklist

  • Confirm hours, and set special hours ahead of holidays, storms and slow-season closures.
  • Add a few new photos.
  • Publish an update about an offer, event or seasonal service.
  • Reply to new reviews.
  • Check for suggested edits from the public or from Google and correct anything wrong.
  • Look at the performance report to see which searches find you and how many calls and direction requests you get.

A complete profile gets you in the running. Steady, accurate upkeep is what keeps you there.

Where the profile fits in your plan

The profile works best alongside a fast website with a clear page for each service and town you cover, which is also what AI assistants read when they recommend local businesses. Google Business Profile questions

Is a Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Creating and managing a Google Business Profile costs nothing. Google may ask you to verify that you run the business before the profile shows publicly.

Can I hide my home address on my profile?

Yes. If you serve customers at their location and don't welcome visitors at your address, you can set up as a service-area business, hide the street address and list the towns you serve instead.

How many categories should I choose?

Pick one primary category that describes what you are, then add a few secondary categories only for services you really offer. The primary category carries the most weight.

Can I offer a discount in exchange for reviews?

No. Google's policies prohibit offering incentives for reviews and ask businesses not to selectively request only positive ones. Ask every customer the same way and reply to every review.

How often should I update my profile?

Check it at least once a month, add new photos regularly, and set special hours ahead of holidays and closures so customers aren't sent to a locked door.
