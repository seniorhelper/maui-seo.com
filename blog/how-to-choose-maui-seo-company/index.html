Hiring helpHow to choose a Maui SEO company

By Zachary Tye Wennstedt. Published September 21, 2026. About 15 minutes to read.

The questions to ask, the red flags Google itself warns about, and a scorecard for comparing proposals. Written so you can hire well, whoever you hire.

The short answer: choose a Maui SEO company that explains exactly what it will do, understands how the island's two audiences and seasons work, reports on calls and customers rather than just rankings, lets you own every account and asset, and never guarantees rankings. Google's own hiring guidance, updated in June 2026, warns against anyone who promises a #1 spot or claims to be "approved" by Google.

Why this choice matters more on Maui

Hiring an SEO company is a bit like hiring a contractor. Most of the work happens where you can't easily see it, the results take a while to show, and by the time you realize the job was done badly, you've already paid for months of it.

On Maui, the stakes are a little higher. Three things make SEO here different from a typical mainland market:

  • Two audiences at once. You're serving residents who search "near me" and visitors planning from thousands of miles away. A strategy built for only one misses half your customers.
  • Strong seasons. Demand swings with winter, whale season and summer travel. SEO that isn't planned months ahead of each season shows up too late.
  • A small market with a long memory. Shortcuts like fake reviews or spammy tactics don't just risk penalties from Google. On an island, they risk your reputation with the people you see at the grocery store.

Add AI search to the mix, with travelers asking ChatGPT and Google's AI to plan whole trips, and the question isn't just "who can get me ranked?" It's "who actually understands how customers find businesses here now?"

What good SEO work actually includes

Before comparing companies, it helps to know what you're buying. Real local SEO isn't one thing. It's a set of connected jobs, and a good company should be able to explain each one in plain English.

Technical health

Making sure search engines can crawl, understand and index your site. That means fixing broken pages, slow load times, missing titles and mobile problems. It's the foundation, and it's often where cheap SEO skips straight past.

Google Business Profile

For local searches, your profile drives the map results and many AI answers. Correct categories, accurate hours, real photos, services, and regular updates all matter. For many Maui businesses, this alone moves the needle more than anything else.

Content that answers real questions

Pages built around what your customers actually ask, written with genuine local knowledge. Not generic articles churned out to hit a word count, and not copy-and-paste pages for every town on the island.

Local consistency

Your name, address and phone number matching across directories, maps and social platforms. Inconsistent details make Google and AI tools less confident about recommending you.

Reviews

A steady system for earning genuine reviews and responding to them. Reviews influence rankings, conversions and AI recommendations all at once.

Reputation and links

Earning mentions and links from real, relevant sources, like local news, community organizations, partners and industry sites. Not buying links from networks built to game rankings.

AI search visibility

Making your business easy for AI tools to understand and cite: clear answers, consistent facts, and nothing blocking the crawlers that power AI search. Our guide on getting recommended by AI covers this in depth.

Tracking and reporting

Measuring calls, forms and bookings, not just traffic, so you know whether the work is paying off.

What Google itself says about hiring an SEO

You don't have to take any agency's word for how to hire well, including ours. Google publishes its own guidance, called "Do you need an SEO?", and it was revised in June 2026 alongside a new page on evaluating third-party SEO tools and advice.

A few points from Google's guidance are worth knowing before you talk to anyone:

  • No one can guarantee a #1 ranking. Google says to be wary of anyone claiming to guarantee rankings, a special relationship with Google, or a "priority submit." There's no such thing.
  • You can submit your own site. Google notes that submitting a site is something you can do yourself, through tools like Search Console and sitemaps.
  • Be careful with secrecy. If a company won't clearly explain what it intends to do, ask. Deceptive tactics done on your behalf can get your site removed from Google.
  • "Approved by Google" is a red flag. According to Search Engine Land's coverage of the update, Google says it doesn't evaluate or endorse third-party SEO tools, those tools don't have access to its internal ranking data, and you should be wary of anything claiming to be approved by Google. The new guidance specifically covers services promising results in AI search, sometimes sold as AEO or GEO.
  • Search Console is the source of truth. Google recommends its own Search Console for data that comes directly from Google Search.

If a pitch sounds like it contradicts Google's own advice, trust Google.

Red flags to walk away from

Some warning signs are subtle. These aren't.

Guaranteed rankings

Nobody controls Google's results. A guarantee is either a misunderstanding of how search works or a sign the company plans to use tactics that could get you penalized.

They keep your assets

If the agency registers your domain, owns your Google Business Profile, or builds your site on a platform only they can access, you're a hostage. Leaving becomes expensive or impossible.

Fake reviews in any form

Writing reviews, buying them, trading them or filtering unhappy customers away from review sites can violate platform rules and damage your reputation when discovered. On an island, it will be discovered.

Mass-produced town pages

Twenty pages that are identical except for the town name look like a lot of work. They usually aren't valuable to customers, and Google can treat them as low-quality or doorway pages.

"We have a special relationship with Google"

Google has said plainly that no such arrangement exists for organic search. Neither does "priority submission."

Secret methods

A company that can't or won't explain what it's doing is either hiding low effort or hiding risk. You deserve to understand what you pay for.

Reports built to look busy

Pages of charts about impressions and keyword positions, with no mention of calls or customers, can mask a campaign that isn't working.

Unsolicited "your site is broken" emails

Google's guidance compares unsolicited SEO emails to the diet-pill spam in your inbox. Good companies rarely find clients by cold-emailing scary audits.

Green flags worth paying for

  • They ask about your business first. Your best customers, your busy months, your margins, before talking tactics.
  • They explain things simply. If they can make SEO make sense to you, they probably understand it well.
  • They tell you what not to do. An honest "that's not worth your money" is worth a lot.
  • They show real, checkable work. Live sites you can search for yourself.
  • They put you in control. Your accounts, your data, your decisions.
  • They care about the whole path to a customer. Rankings, yes, but also whether the website converts and the phone gets answered.

Local knowledge vs a local address

It's natural to assume a company based on Maui will do better SEO for a Maui business. Sometimes that's true. But what actually matters is whether the team understands how Maui customers search and decide: the resident and visitor split, the seasons, the towns, the culture, the correct spelling of place names, and the sensitivity of topics like Lahaina's recovery.

Test for that knowledge directly. Ask how they'd approach a Kīhei condo versus a Wailuku accounting firm. Ask what changes for a tour operator in whale season. Ask how they'd handle a West Maui business that relocated. The answers will tell you quickly whether they know the island or just know SEO.

Equally, a mainland agency that treats Maui like any other zip code will miss what makes local search here different. The right choice is a team that combines real SEO skill with real understanding of the place.

What SEO services cost, and why

SEO pricing varies a lot, and it's hard to compare quotes that include different things. Most companies charge in one of three ways:

  • Monthly retainer. The most common model for ongoing local SEO. Budgets for small businesses commonly range from a few hundred to several thousand dollars a month.
  • Project fee. For a defined job, like an audit, a site migration or a Google profile cleanup.
  • Hourly consulting. For owners who want expert guidance but do the work themselves.

What drives the price up or down: how competitive your searches are, how many services and areas you want to rank for, how much content needs creating, the state of your website, and whether reporting and strategy are included. A very cheap monthly price often means very little actual work. A very expensive one should come with a very clear plan.

The better comparison isn't price. It's what you get for it, and whether it's measured in customers. The marketing planner on our agency page can help you think about where a budget does the most good.

Contracts, ownership and leaving well

Read the agreement before you sign, and make sure it answers these clearly:

  1. What's delivered each month, in specifics.
  2. How long you're committed, and how to cancel.
  3. Who owns everything: domain, website, content, Google Business Profile, analytics and ad accounts. The answer should be you.
  4. What happens to your site if you leave, especially if the company built it.
  5. Access. You should have admin access to your own accounts at all times.

A good SEO company doesn't need to trap you. It keeps you by getting results. If you're leasing a website, as some of our clients do, the terms around ownership and buyout should be spelled out just as clearly. Ours are on the Maui web design page.

What the first 90 days should look like

SEO takes time, but the early months shouldn't be a mystery. Here's a reasonable picture of what a good company does first:

Month one: understand and fix

A full audit of your site, Google profile, reviews, listings, competitors and AI presence. Technical fixes. Google Business Profile cleanup. Tracking set up so calls and forms are counted.

Month two: build

Improved or new pages based on what customers search. Listing consistency work. A review system running. Early content planned for your next busy season.

Month three: expand and report

More content, reputation building, refinements based on early data, and a clear report on what's changed and what's next.

You may see early wins, especially from profile and technical fixes, within weeks. Meaningful organic growth usually takes three to six months, longer in competitive categories. Anyone promising dramatic results in the first month deserves a follow-up question: how?

How to tell if your SEO company is working

Ask for these, and look at them yourself when you can:

  • Google Search Console: clicks, impressions and the searches you appear for, straight from Google.
  • Google Business Profile performance: calls, direction requests and website clicks from your listing.
  • Leads: calls, forms and bookings, ideally tracked to their source.
  • Reviews: steady growth in recent, genuine reviews.
  • Seasonal comparisons: this January against last January, not January against September.

If the numbers that matter aren't improving after a reasonable period, ask why, and expect a straight answer.

Before you hire: are you ready?

The best SEO company in the world can only do so much if a few basics aren't in place on your side. Before you sign with anyone, check these five things. None of them cost money, and each one makes whatever you spend on SEO work harder.

  • You can answer the phone. SEO brings calls. If calls go to a full voicemail box or ring out during busy hours, new customers go to the next result. A missed-call text-back or a simple answering service can be worth more than a month of SEO.
  • You have access to your own accounts. Find your domain login, your website login and your Google Business Profile access now. Recovering lost accounts can delay a campaign by weeks.
  • You know your best customers. Which jobs, bookings or orders are most profitable? That's where SEO effort should go first, and a good company will ask.
  • You're willing to ask for reviews. No agency can earn reviews for you. They can build the system, but you or your team have to make the ask.
  • You'll give it time. If you need calls this week, start with paid advertising while SEO builds. Expecting SEO alone to fix a slow month is how good campaigns get cancelled right before they pay off.

If all five are in place, you're ready to get real value from an SEO company. If not, fix those first. It's the cheapest marketing you'll ever do.

Choosing an SEO company: questions

How do I choose an SEO company on Maui?

Look for a company that explains exactly what it will do, understands Maui's residents, visitors and seasons, measures success in calls and bookings, lets you own all your accounts, and never guarantees rankings.

Can an SEO company guarantee first-page rankings?

No. Google's own guidance says no one can guarantee a #1 ranking and warns against anyone claiming a special relationship with Google or a priority submission.

How much do SEO services cost for a small Maui business?

Ongoing local SEO is usually a monthly retainer, commonly from a few hundred to several thousand dollars a month depending on competition, scope and content needs. Compare what's delivered, not just the price.

How long does SEO take to work?

Early wins from Google Business Profile and technical fixes can show within weeks. Meaningful organic growth usually takes three to six months, and longer in competitive categories.

Should I own my website and Google Business Profile?

Yes, always. Your domain, website, Google Business Profile, analytics and ad accounts should be registered to you, with admin access at all times, so you keep everything if you change companies.

Do I need a Maui-based SEO company?

What matters most is real understanding of how Maui customers search and decide, combined with strong SEO skill. Test that knowledge by asking how they'd approach different Maui businesses and seasons.
