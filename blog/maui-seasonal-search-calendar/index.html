PlanningMaui's seasonal search calendar

By Zachary Tye Wennstedt. Published September 20, 2026. About 12 minutes to read.

When visitors plan, when they arrive, and what to publish, promote and budget each month. With real 2025 visitor numbers, not guesses.

The short answer: Maui visitor traffic peaks around the winter holidays and whale season, stays strong into spring and summer, and dips in early fall. But visitors search weeks or months before they arrive, so your marketing for each season has to go live well ahead of it.

What the 2025 numbers show

Seasonality talk is usually vague. Let's use real numbers. The Hawaiʻi Department of Business, Economic Development and Tourism (DBEDT) publishes monthly visitor statistics. Here are five months of 2025 statewide arrivals from its preliminary reports.

The pattern is clear even in five data points: a strong March, a strong summer and holiday season, and a noticeable September dip. For the full year, DBEDT's preliminary data showed 9.64 million visitors in 2025, still below the 10.4 million of 2019.

Maui has its own story, shaped by recovery from the August 2023 wildfires. Here are three months of Maui arrivals from DBEDT data reported by Maui Now.

Maui visitor arrivals, 2025
Month (2025)Visitors to Maui
January202,738
April207,522
July235,529

July was the strongest of the three, and Maui welcomed 1,503,907 visitors in the first seven months of 2025, according to the same July report. On an average April day, about 51,804 visitors were on the island. That's the audience your marketing is trying to reach, every single day.

A note on honesty: these are preliminary figures for selected months, shown as reported. Your own business data, like calls and bookings by month, is the best guide to your personal seasons.

The lead-time rule

Here's the part most businesses miss. Visitors arriving in February started searching in the fall. Families arriving in July started planning in spring. If your whale-season page goes live in January, you've already missed many of the people who booked in November.

Market to the planner before the season, and to the visitor during it.

A simple way to think about it: publish and rank content for a season two to three months before it starts, turn up paid ads as the season begins, and keep local, resident-focused marketing steady all year.

Whale season deserves its own plan

Humpback whales, koholā in Hawaiian, winter in the warm, shallow waters around Maui. Sightings generally run from about December through April, with the busiest months in the middle of that window. For tour operators, lodging and restaurants near the South and West Maui coasts, it's a season inside the season.

  • Publish early. Whale-watching content should be live and ranking by early fall.
  • Be specific. Departure points, best times of day, what families should bring. Specific pages beat generic ones for people and for AI answers.
  • Update constantly. Fresh sighting reports and photos signal an active, trustworthy business.

Don't forget the residents

Visitors come and go. Residents are there in September too. Businesses that keep a steady base of local SEO, reviews and community presence ride out the quiet months far better than ones that only chase visitor peaks. If you're resident-focused, the calendar matters less than consistency.

Calendars by industry

The island-wide pattern is a starting point. Here's how it tends to play out for different kinds of businesses.

Tours and activities

Whale season makes winter the headline for many ocean tours, with summer family travel close behind. Planning searches start months early, so seasonal tour pages should be live by fall for winter and by spring for summer. Weather affects bookings daily, so clear cancellation policies and fresh condition updates matter all year.

Restaurants

Visitor-heavy areas like Kāʻanapali, Wailea and Kīhei follow the visitor curve closely. Restaurants in Wailuku, Kahului and Upcountry lean more on residents and hold steadier through the year. Everyone benefits from keeping holiday hours accurate on their Google profile, since a closed door on a holiday frustrates both locals and visitors.

Lodging and vacation rentals

Booking windows are long, so marketing runs well ahead of arrivals. Early fall is often the time to push winter availability, and late winter to push summer. Shoulder-season deals help fill the quieter months, especially early fall.

Trades and home services

Demand follows residents and property owners more than visitors. Condo and rental turnover can bring bursts of work between busy seasons, and weather events drive urgent calls. A steady year-round presence usually beats seasonal spikes.

Retail and galleries

Visitor-facing shops follow the visitor curve, with the holidays adding gift shopping on top. Resident-focused retail sees its own bumps around holidays, graduation season and back-to-school.

Content ideas for each season

Seasonal content helps you rank for timely searches and shows customers you're active. A few ideas to adapt:

  • Before winter: a whale-season guide related to your business, holiday hours, gift ideas, what to pack or expect.
  • Before spring: family-friendly activities, spring break tips, planning guides for summer trips.
  • Before summer: kid-friendly options, summer hours, heat and sun safety tips related to what you do.
  • Before fall: resident specials, local events, quieter-season perks for visitors who travel off-peak.

A holiday hours checklist

Wrong hours on a holiday are one of the most common, and most avoidable, ways to lose customers. Before each holiday listed in the calendar above:

  1. Decide your hours early.
  2. Update your Google Business Profile's special hours.
  3. Update your website, ideally with a banner near the top.
  4. Update Facebook, Instagram, Yelp and any booking platforms.
  5. Post a short social update so regulars know.

It takes fifteen minutes and saves a lot of disappointed customers.

Budgeting with the seasons

Spending the same amount every month is the most common budget mistake we see. A smarter pattern:

  • Before a peak: invest in content, SEO and your website.
  • During a peak: spend on paid ads where demand is highest.
  • In a lull: pull back paid spend, focus on residents, reviews and social.

Seasonal marketing questions

When is the busiest season on Maui?

Visitor traffic is generally strongest around the winter holidays, through whale season into spring, and again in summer. Early fall is often the quietest stretch, as the 2025 statewide numbers show with a September dip.

When is whale season on Maui?

Humpback whale sightings generally run from about December through April, with the busiest months in the middle of that window.

How far ahead should I market for a season?

Publish and rank content for a season about two to three months before it starts, then increase paid ads as the season begins. Visitors often plan and book well before they arrive.

Where do these visitor numbers come from?

They're preliminary figures from the Hawaiʻi Department of Business, Economic Development and Tourism, as published by DBEDT and reported by local news outlets. Sources are linked under each chart.

Which Maui businesses are least affected by seasons?

Resident-focused businesses like trades, healthcare and professional services tend to be steadier through the year, while visitor-facing tours, lodging and resort-area restaurants follow the visitor curve more closely.

How do I update holiday hours on Google?

In your Google Business Profile, add special hours for the holiday date. Then match them on your website, social pages and any booking platforms so every source agrees.
