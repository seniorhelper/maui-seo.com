The Hawaiʻi settings mainland ad managers miss

These aren't exotic tricks. They're basic settings that quietly go wrong when an account is set up from the mainland, and they cost real money every day.

The account runs on mainland time

A Google Ads account's time zone is chosen when the account is created, and it isn't something you can freely change later. If it was set to Pacific or Mountain time, every ad schedule is shifted. Your "8 a.m. to 6 p.m." ads may actually run from 5 a.m. to 3 p.m. Hawaiʻi time, and because Hawaiʻi skips daylight saving, the gap even changes during the year.

We check the account time zone first and build schedules around real Hawaiʻi hours.

Targeting the wrong people

Google's location settings offer a choice between reaching people who are in or regularly in your area, and people who are there or have shown interest in it. For a Kahului plumber, "in the area" is right; nobody in Ohio needs a water heater fixed on Maui. For a Kāʻanapali snorkel charter, including people interested in Maui is exactly how you reach planners before they arrive.

Pick the wrong one and you either miss your best customers or pay for clicks that can never convert.

Counting clicks instead of calls

A click isn't a customer. We set up conversion tracking for forms and bookings, and call tracking so phone calls from ads get counted too. Then we optimize toward the actions that actually make you money.

Sending ads to the homepage

Someone who searched "whale watching tour Lahaina" and lands on a generic homepage has to hunt for what they wanted. Most won't. Each campaign gets a page that matches the search it came from.

The same budget all year

Maui demand swings. Spending evenly across every month means overspending in slow weeks and running out of budget in peak season. We plan budgets around your busy and quiet months.

No negative keywords

Without them, a luxury rental can pay for "cheap Maui camping" clicks, and a tour company can pay for "Maui jobs." We prune those constantly.

Ads work better next to SEO

Ads and SEO aren't rivals. They cover each other's weak spots.

Competitive visitor searches, like tours and lodging, generally cost more per click than resident searches for many local services. We'll show you estimated costs for your actual keywords before you spend anything.

Anatomy of a well-built Maui ad account

A tidy account structure is the difference between knowing what works and guessing. Here's how we organize campaigns so every dollar can be traced.

Campaigns by goal and audience

We separate campaigns by what they're trying to do and who they're reaching. Resident searches and visitor searches usually get separate campaigns, because they need different targeting, budgets and messages. A plumber's emergency calls and planned remodels are separate too, because one is urgent and the other is researched.

Tight groups of related searches

Inside each campaign, we group closely related searches together, like "water heater repair" and "water heater replacement," so the ad and landing page can speak directly to what the person typed.

Ads that match the search

Each group gets ads written for those exact searches, with your location, a clear offer and a reason to choose you. Generic ads that could belong to anyone perform worse and cost more.

Extras that earn more clicks

Google lets ads show extra details, called assets: your phone number, your location, links to specific pages, short callouts like "Licensed and insured" or "Small groups." They make ads bigger and more useful at no extra cost per click, and we use every one that fits.

Landing pages built for the ad

Every campaign sends people to a page that continues the conversation the ad started, with the same offer, the same wording and one obvious next step.

Tracking at every step

Calls, forms and bookings are tracked as conversions, so we can see which searches, ads and pages actually produce customers, then move money toward them.

Display and remarketing, explained

Search ads catch people who are already looking. Display ads and remarketing reach people at other moments, and on Maui, those other moments matter a lot.

Display ads

Display ads are image ads that appear on websites and apps across the web. For Maui businesses, they're most useful for reaching people who are interested in Maui travel, before they've decided what to book. They build familiarity, so your name rings a bell when the planner starts searching.

Remarketing

Remarketing shows ads to people who already visited your website. A planner who looked at your snorkel tour page three weeks before their trip might see a reminder as they finalize plans. Because Maui trips are often researched over weeks, remarketing tends to be one of the most efficient ways to recover bookings you'd otherwise lose.

Doing it respectfully

Nobody likes being followed around the internet by the same ad. We limit how often each person sees your ads, stop showing them after someone books, and keep the creative fresh. Remarketing also relies on tracking, which should be explained honestly on your site's privacy page.

How to read your ad report

Ad reports are full of abbreviations. Here's what the important ones mean, and which ones actually matter to your bottom line.

TermWhat it meansHow much it matters
ImpressionsHow many times your ad was shownLow. Visibility isn't business.
ClicksHow many times someone clicked your adMedium. A step toward a customer.
CTR (click-through rate)Clicks divided by impressionsMedium. Shows whether ads are relevant.
CPC (cost per click)What you pay, on average, for each clickMedium. Useful for budgeting.
ConversionsCalls, forms or bookings from adsHigh. These are real leads.
Conversion rateConversions divided by clicksHigh. Shows how well your page converts.
Cost per conversionAd spend divided by conversionsHighest. What each lead actually costs you.

If your current reports focus on the top of that table and skip the bottom, you're seeing activity, not results. We report cost per call or booking first, because that's the number that tells you whether ads are worth it.

A seasonal ad budget, in practice

Here's how a visitor-facing Maui business might shape its ad budget across the year. Your pattern will depend on your own busy and slow months.

  • Late fall: start ramping up for winter and whale season as planners book.
  • Winter: peak spend on your highest-converting searches.
  • Spring: steady spend through spring break, then ease off.
  • Early summer: ramp up again for family travel.
  • Early fall: lowest spend; shift focus to residents and SEO.

Resident-facing businesses usually need a steadier budget, with bumps around the times their own demand rises, like tax season for accountants or the rainy months for roofers.

The point isn't to follow a template. It's to stop spending the same amount in September as in February. Our seasonal search calendar shows the real visitor numbers behind these swings.

Pairing ads with ongoing Maui SEO lets you lean on free organic traffic in quieter months and save ad dollars for when demand peaks.

Maui advertising questions

How fast do Google Ads work for a Maui business?

Ads can start bringing clicks and calls within days of launch, once they're approved. The first few weeks are spent learning which searches convert, so results usually improve over the first month or two.

Why does my Google Ads time zone matter in Hawaiʻi?

Ad schedules follow the account's time zone, which is set when the account is created. If it's on mainland time, your ads run at the wrong hours in Hawaiʻi, and since Hawaiʻi doesn't use daylight saving time, the offset changes during the year.

Should I target people in Maui or people interested in Maui?

Resident-facing businesses usually target people in or near their service area. Visitor-facing businesses often include people who've shown interest in Maui too, so they can reach planners before the trip.

How much should I spend on Google Ads?

It depends on how competitive your searches are and how many customers you want. We estimate costs for your actual keywords first, then recommend a starting budget you can scale up or down with the seasons.

What is remarketing?

Remarketing shows ads to people who already visited your website but didn't call or book. It's especially useful on Maui, where planners often research a trip over several weeks before deciding.

Do I keep my ad account if I stop working with you?

Yes. Your ad account, campaigns and data belong to you.

What's a good cost per lead for a Maui business?

It varies widely by industry and competition. A good cost per lead is one that leaves room for profit given what a customer is worth to you. We track cost per call or booking so you can judge that directly.

What are ad assets?

Assets are extra details Google can show with your ad, like your phone number, location, links to specific pages and short callouts. They make ads more useful and more visible without increasing your cost per click.

Should residents and visitors be in separate campaigns?

Usually, yes. They search differently, need different targeting and respond to different messages, so separating them makes budgets and results much easier to manage.
