Where West Maui stands today

Lahaina and West Maui marketing right now is about one thing: making sure the customers who want to support you can find you, reach you and know you're open. Recovery is happening in stages, and search results often lag behind reality. That gap costs businesses customers every day.

Almost three years after the August 2023 wildfires, the west side is reaching real milestones. Maui County announced that Front Street from Pāpalaua Street to Shaw Street would reopen to vehicles on August 1, 2026, after debris and piling removal, seawall and sidewalk repairs, new railings, tree planting and repaving.

Commercial life is returning too. The County announced that the ʻUlu o Lele marketplace was expected to open in September 2026, supported by a $4 million grant from the Maui Strong Fund, and Maui Now reported it's expected to employ about 90 people.

Visitors have been coming back as well. Hotel occupancy in the Lahaina, Kāʻanapali and Kapalua area reached 66.2% in July 2025, up from 57.9% a year earlier, according to DBEDT figures reported by Maui Now.

Rebuilding is a multi-year process, and much is still changing. But for businesses that are open, reopening or relocated, being findable is no longer something to put off.

The search gap: why customers can't find reopened businesses

When a business closes suddenly, the internet doesn't update itself. Old listings, old addresses and "permanently closed" labels linger for months or years, and they quietly send customers elsewhere.

Here's what we see most often with West Maui businesses:

  • Google still shows the business as closed, or shows the old, destroyed address.
  • Directory listings disagree. Yelp says one thing, TripAdvisor another, Facebook a third.
  • The website is outdated, or was taken down and never replaced.
  • AI answers repeat old information, because that's what they find across the web.
  • Loyal customers don't know you're back. They'd come if they knew.
  • Reviews stopped, so recent searchers see nothing newer than 2023.

Each one is fixable. Together, they can decide whether a returning customer finds you or assumes you're gone.

If your business moved

Many West Maui businesses reopened somewhere new. Moving is where search visibility is easiest to lose, and easiest to protect if it's handled right.

Update, don't start over

In most cases, it's better to update your existing Google Business Profile with the new address than to create a new one. Your reviews, photos and history stay with you. Creating a second profile can split your reviews and confuse Google about which listing is real.

Tell customers where you went

Put your new location at the top of your website and your social pages, in plain words: "We've moved to the Lahaina Cannery Mall" or "Find us now in Kāʻanapali." Describe it the way people navigate, with landmarks.

Update every directory

Old addresses on Yelp, TripAdvisor, Apple Maps, Bing and industry sites keep sending people to the wrong place. We track them down and fix them.

Keep your web address

If your website was taken down, bringing it back at the same domain keeps whatever reputation it had with Google. If page addresses change, we set up redirects so old links still land somewhere useful.

Explain the story, briefly

Many customers want to know what happened and that you're okay. A short, honest note on your about page, written in your own voice, connects with people. Keep it about your business and your community, not the disaster.

Watch for mix-ups

After a move, customers sometimes land on a neighboring business or an old listing. Checking maps and AI answers every few weeks catches problems early.

Marketing with respect

Lahaina is a living community, still grieving and rebuilding at the same time. How businesses talk about the recovery matters, to residents and to the visitors who want to help.

Local leaders and community voices have been clear: visitors are welcome to support open businesses, and the burned areas are not places to sightsee. Good West Maui marketing reflects that.

Our guidelines for every West Maui project

  • Never use images of the fire or burned areas to promote a business.
  • Focus on what's open, what you offer and how to reach you.
  • Be accurate about access, parking and what visitors should expect.
  • Avoid turning the tragedy into a sales hook or slogan.
  • Encourage visitors to spend locally and respect posted access rules.
  • Spell place names correctly, including Lahaina, Kāʻanapali and Honokōwai.
  • Let owners tell their own story, in their own words, if they choose to.

Lahaina and West Maui questions

My business reopened, but Google still says it's closed. What do I do?

Sign in to your Google Business Profile and update its status, hours and address. If you can't access the profile, you can request ownership. We can handle this for you and check that the change actually shows up in search and maps.

Should I create a new Google listing after moving?

Usually not. Updating your existing profile with the new address keeps your reviews and history. A second listing can split your reviews and confuse Google.

How long does it take for Google to show my new information?

Changes to your Google Business Profile often appear within days, though some take longer to review. Directory sites and AI answers can take weeks to catch up, which is why fixing every source matters.

Can you help if my website was taken down?

Yes. We can rebuild it at the same web address to keep whatever standing it had with Google, and set up redirects so old links still work. You can buy the new site outright or lease it from $99 a month.

How should my business talk about the recovery?

Keep the focus on what you offer and how to reach you. A brief, honest note in your own words can connect with customers, but avoid using fire imagery or turning the tragedy into a sales message.

Is Lahaina open to visitors?

Many West Maui businesses are open and welcoming customers, and Front Street reopened to vehicles on August 1, 2026. Rebuilding continues, and fenced and private areas remain off-limits. Visitors are encouraged to support open businesses and follow posted access rules.
